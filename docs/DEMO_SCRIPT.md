## The problem

- How many people actually use a blockchain?

- But an account can be a person, or a program.

  On screen: *A transaction won't tell you which.*

## The idea

- There's one thing programs never do. They never sleep.  
  On screen: *Programs never sleep.*
- So we built a test called Proof of Sleep. It looks at what time each account acts, day after day.  
  On screen: *Proof of Sleep looks at what time each account acts, day after day.*

## See it work

- Here are three real accounts. Each row is a day. Each mark is an hour with activity.  
  On screen: *Each row is a day. Each mark is an hour with activity.*
- This one never touches the same nine hours, and is active at many other times. That's a sleeper.  
  On screen: *Never active in the same 9 hours, active at many others: a sleeper.*
- This one fills nearly every hour. That's a machine.  
  On screen: *Nearly every hour, every day: a machine.*
- And this one fires at the same hour, every day. That's a timer.  
  On screen: *The same hour, every day: a timer.*
- Now let's test a real account, live.  
  On screen: *Now a real account, live.*
- One click, and it reads the account's public history.  
  On screen: *It reads the account's public history.*
- Too fast to be a person.  
  On screen: *Too fast to be a person.*

## What we found

- In October, only about sixty-two thousand of those accounts were active on five days or more. About five thousand eight hundred pass the test.  
  On screen: *61,957 accounts were regular. 5,771 pass the test.*
- That isn't a head count. The test misses many real people, and some accounts pass by chance.  
  On screen: *That isn't a head count. The true number could be far higher or lower.*
- Three hundred forty accounts that never sleep sent seventy-four percent of all transactions.  
  On screen: *340 accounts that never sleep sent 74% of all transactions.*
- And since June twenty twenty-four, the accounts that pass have fallen ninety-five percent.  
  On screen: *Since June 2024, the accounts that pass have fallen 95%.*

## Can you trust it?

- We checked the test against five thousand accounts verified as human by a face scan. Depending on the year, between one in three and one in seven pass. Accounts with shuffled days pass about two in a hundred.  
  On screen: *Face-verified humans: between 1 in 3 and 1 in 7 pass. Shuffled accounts: about 2 in 100.*
- So a pass is real evidence, but the test misses most people.  
  On screen: *A pass is real evidence, but the test misses most people.*
- People who swap through NEAR Intents, or use relayers, often never sign for themselves. So we can't see them all.  
  On screen: *People who swap through NEAR Intents, or use relayers, are harder to see.*
- And when clocks changed, we saw no shift. So we can't prove these accounts keep local time.  
  On screen: *When clocks changed, we saw no shift.*

## What it means

- So here is the thesis. Don't treat the user counts on NEAR as proof of demand. Value NEAR for the money it moves, and treat real people as the upside.  
  On screen: *Don't read NEAR's user counts as proof of demand. Value NEAR for the money it moves.*
- We list the exact numbers that would change our minds.  
  On screen: *We list the numbers that would change our minds.*
