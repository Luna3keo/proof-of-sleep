# Proof of Sleep: the NEAR thesis

Every number in this file is generated by `python rebuild.py` from `data/raw/`. Nothing is typed in by hand.

## The claim

**Over the next five years, NEAR should be valued as settlement infrastructure run by machines, not as a consumer network whose worth can be read from its user counts.**

For the two weeks of 1-14 October 2026:

- 748,667 accounts were active, but 668,994 of them showed up on only one or two days (it was 60,870 the month before). Only 61,957 were active on at least 5 of the 14 days.
- 5,771 of those regular accounts pass a strict sleep test. That is not a head count: the test misses most verified humans, and some accounts pass by chance. Depending on two rates that cannot be measured precisely, the true number of regular people could be anywhere from about 1,200 to about 34,400.
- 340 accounts that never sleep sent 74% of all transactions. Of the 100 busiest accounts, 99 are machines and 0 sleep.
- Accounts passing the test have fallen 95% since June 2024, while two-week swap volume through NEAR Intents went from $1.1M to $2B since January 2025.

This is a thesis about which number to believe. It is not a price target, and no model here maps people to a token price.

## Why this matters for a long-term holder

An investor in a layer-one is buying future demand for that chain. User counts are the evidence most often offered for that demand. If those counts are mostly machines and one-off bursts, any valuation built on them is empty, and the real question becomes whether the machine-run flow is worth owning. This work removes one argument and gives a measurement to replace it. It does not try to answer whether the flow pays holders.

## The hypothesis tested

The starter prompt for this track suggested that NEAR Intents volume is a better leading indicator of real usage than price. I replaced it with a test that comes first: **how much of NEAR's reported usage looks like people at all?** People sleep and machines do not, so an account's daily rhythm is evidence about what is behind it. [METHOD.md](METHOD.md) has the details.

## Evidence for

1. **Active-account counts swing on bursts.** October's count of 748,667 includes 668,994 accounts seen on only one or two days. In a typical window from March 2025 to September 2026, 5 of every 100 active accounts passed the test. The number passing and the headline count barely move together: their monthly changes correlate at 0.19. *(Dashboard: "Active accounts or actual people?")*
2. **Regular users are far fewer than active accounts.** Of 748,667 active accounts, 61,957 were active on at least 5 of 14 days.
3. **Transactions are mostly machine traffic.** 340 accounts that never sleep sent 74% of all transactions. The busiest 100 accounts sent 72%. Some of this is relayers acting for others: delegated actions from accounts that do not themselves look like machines were 13% of all transactions. *("Who actually sends the transactions?")*
4. **The accounts that pass are concentrated.** 84% are Telegram accounts and 86% use one game, `game.hot.tg`. A base that narrow rises and falls with one product. *("Who passes the test?", "What do these accounts use?")*
5. **The accounts that pass are declining.** Down 95% from 111,138 in June 2024 to 5,771. Over the last 12 windows the count fell 12.4% a month, a half-life of about 5 months. Allowing for verified humans passing less often than they used to, the fall is still about 92%.
6. **Money moved while people did not.** Two-week Intents volume went from $1.1M in January 2025 to $2B. Machines signed 99% of the 927,966 transactions sent to `intents.near` in the latest window. *("Did value follow the people?")*
7. **The test separates real people from chance.** 25% of the windows from face-verified human accounts pass, against 1.9% for accounts whose days were shuffled around the clock. Verified humans go quiet for about 7 hours, close to a night's sleep. Raw transaction counts match the independent NearBlocks indexer within 1% in 70 of 70 windows and 69 of 69 days. *("Does the test find real people?")*

## Evidence against

These cut against the claim. They are as much a part of this thesis as the list above.

1. **The test cannot give a head count.** Verified humans pass 25% of the time over all years, 15% in the last 12 windows, so it misses most real people. About 21% of the accounts that pass could be chance. No labelled set of bots exists, so nobody knows how often a scripted account passes. Across plausible values the true number of regular people runs from about 1,200 to about 34,400.
2. **Verified humans pass less often than they used to**: 33% in 2023, 22% in 2024, 15% in 2026. Part of the fall in the number passing may be people changing how they use NEAR, not leaving it.
3. **Verified humans are only about 1.9 times as likely to pass as a typical regular account** (25% against 13% in the same windows). The test tells people from chance far better than it tells people from everything else.
4. **People behind relayers and people who sign off-chain are hard to see.** Counting relayed actions, the same test adds 4,203 accounts, +73% on top of signers. People using NEAR Intents usually sign off-chain, and nothing here counts them. The consumer thesis may have moved rather than died.
5. **A script that keeps office hours passes as a person.** One person with many accounts also counts many times.
6. **The clock-change experiment found no shift.** Across 4 clock changes, accounts that pass moved -0.05 hours in the expected direction (95% interval -0.26 to +0.16), so a full one-hour move is ruled out. Either few of them live where clocks change, or they do not follow local time. This data cannot separate the two, and I do not count it as support.
7. **Whether accounts that pass outlast machines depends on the period.** Pooled since January 2025, 30% of accounts that pass are still regularly active 12 months later, against 15% of accounts that never sleep. In the three most recent cohorts it goes the other way: 27% against 42%.
8. **Passing the test does not lead the price.** Monthly changes in the number passing do not lead price by 1, 2 or 3 months (best correlation +0.14, p = 0.31). 4 of 28 tests pass p < 0.05, against about 1.4 expected by chance, a borderline excess (chance of that many or more is 5%). They are: the number passing in the same month as price (r = +0.30); active accounts 1 month after price (r = +0.34); transactions in the same month as price (r = +0.31); transactions 3 months before price (r = +0.27). None shows the number passing leading the price.
9. **Part of the decline may be cyclical.** The count fell through a long drawdown in price (low of $1.08 in February 2026).

## What these sources cannot show

No number is estimated or filled in where the data does not support it. The following are not computed:

- **The number of people using NEAR Intents.** Swaps are signed off-chain. The Intents Explorer API needs a partner token, which this project did not have, and its terms limit keeping the data. Swap volume here comes from DefiLlama's public series, and [`pipeline/pull_intents_explorer.py`](pipeline/pull_intents_explorer.py) is provided for anyone with a token. It has not been run against the live API.
- **People's share of fees.** Attributing gas per account across the whole history needs the much larger receipt tables.
- **How many sleeping accounts are one person or one operator.**
- **Where any individual lives.** The time-zone chart describes the crowd.
- **The error rate on scheduled bots**, because no labelled set of them exists.
- **A price target.** No analysis here supports one.

## What would change my mind

| Measure | Today | Would change my mind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accounts passing the sleep test | 5,771 | above 25,000 for three windows in a row |
| Named `.near` accounts that pass | 585 | above 3,000 |
| Share of those passing that are Telegram accounts | 84% | below 50% |
| Accounts that pass only when relayed actions are counted | 4,203 | more than signers, three windows in a row |
| Never-sleeping accounts' share of transactions | 74% | below 50% while transaction counts hold up |
| People using NEAR Intents | not measurable here | a credible count, from a source like the Explorer API, showing a daily human rhythm at scale |

Any one of the first five on its own would make me reconsider. The last would directly reopen the consumer case.

## A five-year view

This is arithmetic, not a forecast, and a single new product could break it.

- At the pace of the last 12 windows (12.4% down a month) the count of accounts passing would be about 1,176 in a year.
- To regain the June 2024 peak by 2031, the count would need to grow +5.1% a month, every month, for five years.
- To reach 25,000 by 2031 it would need to grow +2.5% a month.

The dashboard has a calculator for other assumptions.
